Restaurant Ordering App Redesign

Design Challenge Description

The design challenge pursued in this project was to create a high fidelity mockup of a mobile app for Val's Vegan Kitchen that had complimentary affordances for two kinds of users: customers and employees. Through the app, customers would be able to view the menu and information about the restaurant, place orders, earn rewards, and interact with other customers through a leaderboard. Employees would be able to make stock adjustments, look over sales, and update the app's Ul in order to reflect the current state of the restaurant.

User testing and Significant Results

The user testing plan involves asking the following questions in order to gauge where they
fall on a usability metric.

  1. Have you ever eaten at Val’s Vegan Kitchen?
  1. On a scale of 1-10, how familiar are you with food delivery apps?
  1. Are there any obstacles that prevent you from ordering food online?

Using open ended questions and allowing the user to follow along with the ordering experience to elaborate on any possible obstacles ensures that all the data is unbiased and clear. This also allows for the team member performing the task to take detailed notes of the different scenarios to then analyze.

Issues reported

1: Customer Ordering Experience: User testing revealed several issues within the customer ordering workflow. Both new and returning customers experienced confusion when attempting to customize and place their orders. Participants noted that the combo feature was not consistently available across screens, making it difficult to upgrade items into a combo meal at different points in the ordering process. Additionally, dietary information was not clearly visible while browsing the menu, requiring users to open individual items to determine whether they met their dietary needs. Users also reported uncertainty when selecting between pickup and delivery because the delivery option appeared to be the default choice. This redesign component was selected because these issues directly affected the customer ordering experience and made completing orders less efficient.

2: Client Inventory and Order Management: User testing also identified several issues within the client side inventory and order management workflow. ‘Kitchen staff’ had difficulty finding a clear way to move inventory items between stock categories, such as from ‘In Stock 'Out of Stock.’ Participants also struggled to locate the appropriate controls for updating order delays and notifying customers of changes to preparation times. In addition, users wanted clearer confirmation messages after completing actions to ensure that inventory updates and customer notifications had been successfully applied. This redesign component was selected because these issues affect critical restaurant operations and could lead to inventory inaccuracies, communication errors, and reduced efficiency for staff members.

2 Redesigns discussed for combo making functionality

Option 1: Persistent Combo Functionality

The first redesign option focuses on improving consistency throughout the ordering
process by making the "Make it a Combo" feature available on multiple screens. Rather than
limiting this option to a single point in the workflow, users would be able to convert an item into a combo from the menu page, item detail page, cart page, and checkout page. During user testing, several participants expected the combo option to remain available as they moved through the ordering process and were frustrated when it was not accessible on certain screens. This redesign would create a faster and more flexible ordering experience by reducing the number of steps required to customize a meal. It would also be particularly beneficial for returning customers who already know what they want and prioritize speed and efficiency when placing an order.

Option 2: Guided Combo Builder (chosen by users)

The second redesign option introduces a dedicated combo building workflow. Instead of
selecting a combo through a single button, users would be guided through a step by step process in which they first choose a main item, then a side, followed by a beverage before reviewing their selections and adding the combo to their cart. This approach provides more structure and guidance throughout the ordering process. This redesign would be especially helpful for first time customers who may be unfamiliar with the menu and available combo options. The flow utilizes important selection design like radial buttons to make it clear that this is a “select one” process. The screen is chunked into main, side, and drink, which ensures that the customer is aware of all that they must select before proceeding. By walking users through each selection, the workflow reduces uncertainty and clearly communicates what is included in a combo meal. However, because it introduces additional steps, it may be less efficient for returning customers who are already familiar with the menu and wish to order quickly.

Brief User Test on Redesign Results

How and why option B

After creating both redesign concepts, the participants were shown the redesigned
workflows and asked which option would make the process of ordering a combo meal easier and more intuitive. While participants liked redesign option A, several users noted that it still relied on them noticing and understanding the combo feature on their own. In contrast, redesign option B provided a more structured and guided experience that clearly communicated how a combo meal should be built.
Participants really liked the step by step workflow in option B because it removed
uncertainty from the ordering process. Users felt that separating the combo into a main item, side, and beverage made it easier to understand what was included and ensured that they would not accidentally miss any part of the meal. This was particularly beneficial for first time customers who were unfamiliar with the menu and available combo options. Participants also noted that the organization of the screen made the selection process feel more intuitive and easier to follow. Although option A required fewer clicks, users felt that option B provided a clearer overall experience and better addressed the usability issues identified during testing. The guided workflow reduced confusion, improved visibility of available combo options, and created a more structured ordering process.
Based on the testing results, redesign option B was selected as the final solution. The
redesign was chosen because it provides better guidance throughout the ordering process while reducing uncertainty for users. By walking customers through each component of the combo meal, the workflow creates a more intuitive and user friendly experience that better supports both new and returning customers.

Redesigns Following User Testing: Customer End

Improving the Combo Meal Ordering Experience

User testing revealed that customers struggled to find and understand how to order a combo meal. While users could easily add individual menu items to their cart, the option to convert an item into a combo was unclear and inconsistent. New customers needed guidance on what was included in a combo, while returning customers expected a faster, more streamlined ordering process.

To solve this, our team explored two redesigns: a persistent "Make it a Combo" button available throughout the ordering flow, and a Guided Combo Builder. We selected the Guided Combo Builder because it provided clearer guidance instead of relying on users to notice the combo option.

The final design walks users through three simple steps: select a main, choose a side, and pick a drink. Using clear sections and single-selection controls makes the process easy to understand and ensures users complete their combo before adding it to their cart.

This redesign improved usability by making the combo process more visible, structured, and predictable. It reduced confusion, prevented users from accidentally missing items, and created a faster, more intuitive experience for both first-time and returning customers.

Final Design Discussion and Presentation: Customer End

The final design supports two main user groups of customers ordering food and restaurant staff managing menu operations. On the customer side the app allows users to browse menu categories, build a combo meal, add items to the cart, continue browsing, view promotions, see delivery charges, and receive suggested items that go well with their order. On the restaurant staff side, the app allows employees to view dashboard information, manage sales data, update inventory availability, adjust preparation time, pause orders, and control menu related details. Together these features make the app function as both a customer ordering system and a restaurant management tool.
